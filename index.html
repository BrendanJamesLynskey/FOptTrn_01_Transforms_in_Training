What a Training Step Adds to Inference

The inference series asked what share of a forward pass a Fourier-optical transform engine could take (FOptInf 02). Training runs the same forward pass and then two more kinds of work:

PhaseFor a layer y = W xFLOPs, matmul layerCounted?
Forwardy = W x, keeping x for later2 per weight per tokenyes
Backward, dXthe activation gradient WT G, passed to the layer below2 per weight per tokenyes
Backward, dWthe weight gradient G XT, summed over every token of the step2 per weight per tokenyes
OptimizerAdamW: a few element-wise operations per parameter, once per stepper parameter, not per tokenno (as norms are not)
  • The step is large. The analysis takes an optimizer step of 1,048,576 tokens (illustrative, about what Llama-scale runs use), so at 2,048 tokens a step is 512 sequences. Anything done once per step is amortised over all of them.
  • The weights change after every step. An inference engine writes its Fourier-plane masks once; a training engine must rewrite them (slide 11).
  • The question for this deck: which of the new work is a transform, which of it could sit on a mask, and does the share go up or down? Every number comes from analysis/train_flop_share.py, which extends the inference ledger with the backward pass and writes results.md. Same model shape (Llama-3-8B), same FLOP conventions (matmul 2mnk, real FFT 2.5 n log2 n, complex multiply 6, add 2).
02

The 6N Rule, Reproduced

Before trusting the ledger on new layers, check it on the old one. Training a dense model costs about 6N FLOPs per token (2 forward, 2 for dX, 2 for dW), plus an attention term that grows with the sequence (Kaplan et al., arXiv:2001.08361; the same accounting appears in the Chinchilla paper's appendix, Hoffmann et al., arXiv:2203.15556). Here N counts the parameters that multiply activations: the 8,029,995,008 of the untied model less the 128,256 × 4,096 input embedding, which is a lookup.

Source: results.md, section “1. Cross-check: the 6N rule”; the pytest suite checks the ratio to 1e-12.

  • The match is exact by construction: the ledger charges every matmul's backward at twice its forward, which is what the rule says. Its value is as a check on the bookkeeping (no layer counted twice or missed).
  • Not counted, as in the rule: softmax, norms, activations, the AdamW update and activation recomputation. FlashAttention's backward recomputes the attention scores (Dao et al., arXiv:2205.14135), and activation checkpointing re-runs parts of the forward pass (Chen et al., arXiv:1604.06174); both add digital work, which would lower every share in this deck a little further.
03

What the Inference Series Found

FOptInf 02 counted the forward pass. The short version:

A standard decoder has no FFT

A Llama-style transformer is matmuls, attention, norms and activations. A Fourier transform engine (as against an optical MAC, which would speed every matmul) has nothing to do; its share is zero, forward or backward.

Where FFTs appear, they are small

Long-convolution models (Hyena, Poli et al., arXiv:2302.10866) mix tokens by FFT. The inference series found that for a Hyena-2 model of Llama-3-8B's shape the transform work is 0.20% of prefill FLOPs at 2,048 tokens: the FFT is what makes Hyena cheap, and the projections and MLP dominate.

This script's forward column agrees: Hyena-2 at 2,048 tokens, 0.20% of the forward pass (slide 08). The physics of the engine (the lens as a Fourier transformer, the 4f system, masks and SLMs, ENOB) is in FOptInf 01 and is not repeated here.

The hope training might raise

Training has three passes for every one of inference, and FFT convolution was introduced for these models to make training affordable at length. Perhaps the backward pass is where transforms pile up? The next slides derive the backward pass of a long convolution, then count. The answer is no: the share goes down.

04

The Gradient of a Convolution Is a Correlation

One channel of a causal long convolution, length L, with the output gradient g = ∂ℒ/∂y arriving from above:

derivation sketch (real signals, indices outside 0..L−1 are zero)
forward      y[i]   = Σk h[k] u[i−k]                    Y  = H · U
input grad   ∂ℒ/∂u[j] = Σi g[i] ∂y[i]/∂u[j] = Σi g[i] h[i−j]
                                         dU = G · conj(H)
filter grad  ∂ℒ/∂h[k] = Σi g[i] u[i−k]          dH = G · conj(U)
  • Each gradient is a correlation: the output gradient slid against the other operand. By the correlation theorem a correlation of real signals is a product with a conjugate spectrum, so dU = IFFT(G·conj(H)) and dH = IFFT(G·conj(U)).
  • Causal padding carries over. The forward pass is causal (y[i] uses inputs at or before i); its gradient is anti-causal (du[j] uses gradients at or after j), the mirror image. Both need lags from −(L−1) to L−1 free of wrap-around, so the same zero-padding to n ≥ 2L that made the forward FFT linear makes the backward one exact; keep the first L outputs.
  • What it costs per channel: one FFT of G and one inverse FFT for dU (the same two transforms as the forward), the product with conj(H), the product with conj(U) (U's spectrum is kept from the forward pass), and an accumulation. dH is summed over the step's sequences in the Fourier domain and inverse-transformed once per step.

05

Three 4f Passes: Forward, dX and dW

Mapped onto a 4f system (lens, Fourier-plane mask, lens; FOptInf 01, “The 4f System”), one channel of one layer needs three passes per training sequence. What matters is what sits on the mask:

  • Forward and dX have the shape an optical engine wants: a data stream through a mask that holds a weight. The backward-dX mask is the forward mask's conjugate.
  • dW is different in kind. Both operands of G·conj(U) belong to the sequence being processed. Whichever goes on the mask, the mask must be rewritten for every sequence of every layer.
06

Weight-Masked and Data-by-Data Products

The ledger splits every Fourier-domain product by its operands, because that decides whether a mask written once per optimizer step can hold it:

conj(H) can be a mask

  • H is the spectrum of a weight, fixed between optimizer steps. So is conj(H).
  • For a real filter, conj(H(f)) = H(−f): the conjugate spectrum is the same mask point-reflected through the optical axis. A modulator that writes complex values can instead write the negated phase.
  • Equivalently, a correlation is a convolution with the time-reversed filter: the classical optical correlator is a 4f system with the conjugate spectrum on the mask (FOptInf 01). In principle, reversing the gradient at the input and output lets the forward mask itself serve the backward pass (this deck's reasoning, not a demonstrated system).

conj(U)·G cannot be a static mask

  • U is the spectrum of this sequence's input to this channel; G is its output gradient. Neither survives to the next sequence.
  • Putting conj(U) on the mask and passing G through works, but costs a mask write per sequence per layer: minutes of frames per step on one illustrative device, and still about a whole digital step for Hyena-2 when spread over one engine per GPU (slide 11).
  • Optics can correlate two live signals without a written mask (a joint transform correlator records the intensity of their joint spectrum and transforms it again), but that intermediate must itself be written to a modulator for every pair: it moves the write, it does not remove it.
Class in the ledgerProductsMask written
transformFFTs and inverse FFTsnone (the lenses)
weight-maskedU·H forward, G·conj(H) backwardonce per step, per direction
data-by-dataG·conj(U) for the weight gradientonce per sequence (per token for block-circulant)

Static share = transforms plus weight-masked products, over all step FLOPs: what an engine whose masks change only at optimizer steps could take. The optical share adds the data-by-data products as well.

07

Block-Circulant Weights in Training

The inference series' one large-share case made every projection and MLP matrix block-circulant (CirCNN, Ding et al., arXiv:1708.08917; block 256 here, illustrative): each block is defined by one vector, so a block product is an FFT, a pointwise product and an inverse FFT. Training applies the same split per block:

PassPer token, per block row and columnClass
ForwardFFT the input blocks; multiply by each weight block's spectrum Wpq; sum over input blocks; IFFT the output blockstransform, weight-masked
Backward dXFFT the gradient blocks; multiply by conj(Wpq); sum; IFFTtransform, weight-masked
Backward dWmultiply Gp by conj(Xq), accumulated over every token of the step; one IFFT per block per stepdata-by-data
Once per stepFFT of every weight block after the update; IFFT of every block gradienttransform, amortised
  • The weight gradient is now a data-by-data product per token, not per sequence, because every token's input blocks form a new conj(X). At 4,096 tokens, 20.73% of this model's optical work is data-by-data.
  • The LM head stays dense and, with every other matrix circulant, is most of what remains digital; the explorer's bars (slide 10) show it.
Speculative

No published LLM of this size is block-circulant throughout, as far as the inference series found. Whether such a model trains to useful quality is unknown, and training it would itself be the experiment.

09

Why the Share Falls from Forward to Step

Every row of the table has the same shape: backward share well below forward share, step share in between. The ledger shows why, per Hyena channel and per matmul:

WorkForwardBackwardBackward / forward
Long FFTs per channelFFT(u), IFFT(y)FFT(G), IFFT(dU)the same
Fourier-domain products per channelU·HG·conj(H), G·conj(U)twice
Matmuls (projections, MLP, LM head)W xWT G and G XTtwice
  • The backward pass adds no long FFTs beyond the forward's count, because U's spectrum is kept from the forward pass and dH is accumulated in the Fourier domain. For Hyena-2, where FFTs are most of the optical work and matmuls most of the total, the backward share is therefore about half the forward: 0.21% against 0.11% at 4,096 tokens.
  • The step is a weighted mean, about one part forward to two parts backward (for Hyena-2 the backward pass is 1.998x the forward in FLOPs, results.json), so it lands nearer the backward share: 0.15%.
  • Block-circulant falls less (14.18% to 11.16%), because there the Fourier-domain products, which do double, are a large part of the optical work.
  • The per-step terms are negligible. Filter FFTs and gradient IFFTs once per step are shared by 256 sequences at 4,096 tokens, so they are a tiny correction to the per-sequence work.
So

Training does not rescue the transform share. FFT convolution makes long-context training cheap, and that is exactly why it is a small part of what a training step spends.

Masks Rewritten Every Optimizer Step

An inference engine writes its filter spectra once and keeps them. A training engine must rewrite every weight spectrum after every optimizer step, for the forward pass (H) and the backward pass (conj(H)). On the inference series' illustrative device, a 2-megapixel micromirror device at 1,031 Hz with 8-bit depth, holding one complex value per pixel (optimistic):

  • Weight masks alone rival the step. Rewriting the Hyena-2 filter spectra takes 0.52 s per step at 2,048 tokens and 2.08 s at 8,192 (longer sequences have longer filter spectra), against a 2.05 s digital step; for the block-circulant model, whose digital step is only 0.15 s, the rewrite alone is several times the step.
  • Data-by-data masks are out of reach: 133 s of frames per step for Hyena-2, 385 s for block-circulant, against seconds of digital work. The weight gradient stays digital, and only the static share of slide 08 is a candidate.
  • Bigger steps help the weight masks only: their frame count does not depend on how many tokens the step holds, while the digital step time grows with it. The explorer on slide 10 computes this live.
12

Layer-Major Order

The frame counts above assume the most favourable schedule: layer-major order, in which every sequence of the step passes through one layer while that layer's mask is loaded, before the mask is rewritten for the next layer.

Layer-major: one write per layer per step

  • Forward: load layer 1's H, stream all 512 sequences (at 2,048 tokens), move to layer 2, and so on.
  • Backward, in reverse layer order: load conj(H), stream all the gradients.
  • Each weight mask is written twice per step, which is the “fwd + bwd” column of slide 11.

What it costs

  • Activation memory: the whole step's activations at a layer boundary must be held at once, where ordinary training holds one micro-batch's.
  • Pipeline parallelism streams micro-batches through stages in sequence order, the opposite schedule.
  • Sequence-major order instead would reload every mask for every sequence, multiplying the weight-mask frames by the sequences per step.

Whether a cluster can run layer-major for the layers that touch the engine, and what that does to memory, parallelism and step time, is the subject of deck 03, “Optical Engines in a Training Cluster”.

What Does Not Map in Training

WorkWhy a Fourier transform engine does not help
Attention, forward and backwardData-dependent, causal, softmax-normalised products: not convolutions. The backward pass recomputes or stores the score matrix and does twice the forward's matmul work; none of it is a transform
Dense projections, MLP, LM head (dX and dW)General matmuls, now three times over: work for an optical MAC, not a Fourier engine, unless the weights are structured (slide 07)
Weight gradients of Fourier-domain layersData-by-data products (slide 06): a mask write per sequence, or per token for block-circulant
Norms, activations, softmax and the lossNon-linear; stay digital, forward and backward
The optimizer's element-wise workAdamW (Loshchilov and Hutter, arXiv:1711.05101) updates each parameter from its gradient and two moment estimates: element-wise arithmetic, not a transform
AdamW's statesTwo moments per parameter, kept in memory between steps: a capacity and bandwidth problem. A Fourier-plane mask is not a store for them
MoE routingData-dependent dispatch of tokens to experts, with its own load-balancing gradients: control flow, not a convolution
Gradient all-reduceCommunication. DeMo DCT-compresses the momentum before it is sent, but its transforms are 4.76e-06 of a step's FLOPs: the saving is in bytes, the subject of deck 03 and the Compute in Transit series

And the hidden costs that apply even where it maps, now per step instead of once: mask rewrites, conversions per value, and the precision a gradient format asks for (deck 02).

14

What to Take Away

  • The ledger is right where it can be checked: it reproduces the 6N rule plus attention exactly for the transformer, and the spectral backward formulas match direct sums to rounding.
  • The gradient of a convolution is a correlation. dU = IFFT(G·conj(H)) uses the weight's own spectrum, mirrored: a mask that can be written once per step. dH = IFFT(G·conj(U)) multiplies two data spectra: a mask per sequence, so it stays digital.
  • Training does not rescue the transform share. The backward pass has as many long FFTs as the forward while its matmuls double, so the share falls: Hyena-2 at 4,096 tokens is 0.21% of a forward pass, 0.15% of a step and 0.14% with static masks.
  • Block-circulant weights (speculative at LLM scale) raise the step share to 12.19% (Amdahl 1.139x), but a fifth of that optical work is the weight gradient, so once-per-step masks hold 9.66% (1.107x).
  • The masks change every step. Even in layer-major order, rewriting the weight spectra takes from a quarter of a digital step (2,048 tokens) to a whole one (8,192) for Hyena-2 on the illustrative device, and several steps' worth for block-circulant; data-by-data masks would take minutes.
